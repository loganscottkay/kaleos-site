## Ten ways to take KALEOS from great to unforgettable

KALEOS sells one feeling: your judgment, applied at a scale one person could never reach alone, with nothing leaving until you sign. The v2 build has the bones (black field, the K with its lit star, one orbit, outlined words) but most of it still only displays that feeling. These are ways to make the interface behave like it.

1. **Unsigned words sign themselves.** Every cyan-outlined phrase is "unsigned": hollow, waiting. When its section is fully in view the stroke fills to solid white in one clean 600ms pass, as if a signature landed. The whole page becomes a document being approved as you read it.
2. **Hairlines are drawn, not printed.** Every section rule and step divider draws itself left to right as it enters, like a pen stroke. The page is being written in front of the visitor rather than delivered as a template.
3. **The gate pulses when work crosses it.** On the orbit, each time a mark passes the blue ring the halo fires once and a tiny mono counter beside it increments. The diagram stops being an illustration and starts keeping a log.
4. **The wordmark exhales.** KALEOS sits wide-tracked and airy on the hero, and the letter-spacing tightens the moment the page starts to scroll. The brand settles into work mode with the reader.
5. **Buttons lean toward the hand.** The two hero buttons drift a few pixels toward the cursor and settle back when it leaves, a small physical give. On black, with everything else still, it reads as the only warm thing on the page.
6. **The star answers stillness.** The star at the K's pinch brightens when the pointer stops moving and dims while it travels. Attention is rewarded; restlessness is not. Nobody will name what happened, but they will feel watched by something calm.
7. **A shooting star on the signature.** Hovering the final blue button fires one streak across the closing field, from the K toward the button. The sky signs off.
8. **The orbit tilts with the pointer.** A few degrees of 3D tilt on the orbit's plane as the cursor moves, so the ellipse reads as a real orbit seen from an angle, not a drawn oval.
9. **Numerals as constellations.** The step numbers 01 to 04 are set as faint dot-clusters that resolve into the numeral when their column enters view, the same trick as the star field, spent only on four glyphs.
10. **The page remembers the visit.** On the second visit the hero skips the settle, the star is already lit, and the field is denser. Returning readers get a sky that knows them. Too much for most firms, which is the point.
